Of all the stages in restoring water damage, drying the structure counts most, and it is the stage most often botched by people without training. After extraction removes the standing water, significant moisture remains trapped in structural materials, including drywall, wood framing, subflooring, concrete, insulation and other building components. A structure left wet ends up with odors, wood rot, mold and failing materials, so the moisture has to be removed in a scientific way using dehumidification and controlled evaporation.

The structural drying technicians at Salina Restoration are IICRC-certified and trained in psychrometry, the science of how temperature, humidity and moisture in air relate. That knowledge lets them work out where equipment goes, what temperature to hold and how air should move so evaporation is as effective as possible on every job. Plaster, concrete, hardwood and drywall each dry at their own rate, so each needs its own approach to get down to an acceptable moisture level with no added damage.

Every day of the drying process, we check moisture using thermal imaging cameras, thermo-hygrometers and calibrated moisture meters. Readings are recorded from start to finish in a detailed drying log, which shows moisture dropping step by step in every affected material. Those readings guide daily equipment adjustments that keep the drying as efficient as it can be. A surface that feels dry to your hand proves nothing, so the job is finished only once all affected materials test within the acceptable standards set by IICRC S500.

When to Call for Structural Drying in Salina

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Earlier water damage that was never professionally dried and now shows deterioration
  • Wallboard with water stains, puffiness or a spongy feel
  • Trapped moisture often announces itself with a persistent musty odor after cleanup
  • After water damage, a meter shows elevated moisture in the floor, wall or ceiling
  • Surfaces that stay cool or clammy once the standing water has been removed
  • Paint or wallpaper that bubbles, peels or blisters after a leak
  • Window glass that fogs up near the affected rooms

How We Handle Structural Drying

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle structural drying from start to finish:

1

Moisture Assessment

Using moisture meters, thermal imaging and hygrometers, we create a comprehensive moisture map of all affected areas. From that map we decide where equipment goes and record our baseline readings.

2

Setting the Equipment

Our crew sets up high-velocity air movers along with commercial LGR units in the positions the moisture map calls for. The materials being dried determine how we set the airflow and the temperature.

3

Daily Checks and Adjustments

A technician comes back every day, reads moisture at each monitoring point, moves equipment as needed and logs the progress. You receive updates on the drying timeline.

4

Dry Standard Verification

Once every affected material reads at an acceptable moisture level, we record the final numbers, pull the equipment and certify the property as meeting dry standard.

Structural Drying Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Trust the moisture meter readings, because surfaces can feel dry while moisture remains deep in materials
  • Until a technician removes the equipment, keep it running day and night without breaks
  • Leave equipment where the technician placed it, since positions are based on the moisture map
  • If a new damp spot or smell shows up while drying is underway, let the technician know at once
  • Dehumidifiers lose efficiency when outside air gets in, so keep drying areas closed up
  • Do not turn off professional drying equipment to reduce noise, because every hour matters

Why Professional Structural Drying Matters

There is real science behind structural drying, which is why guessing does not work. Secondary mold damage most often traces back to someone who put down fans and hoped things would dry. Professional structural drying requires understanding how different materials absorb and release moisture, calculating the grain depression (moisture removal capacity) each situation needs, and monitoring daily to confirm drying is progressing properly. Lacking that understanding, people either stop too soon and leave moisture for mold, or dry too hard and harm wood and other materials.

The equipment used for professional structural drying is fundamentally different from consumer products. High-velocity air movers produce precise airflow patterns that speed up evaporation from the structure, and low-grain refrigerant (LGR) commercial dehumidifiers capture 10 to 20 times the moisture a home unit can. Between the investment in equipment and the training needed to run it, it is no surprise that DIY drying falls well short of a professional job.
